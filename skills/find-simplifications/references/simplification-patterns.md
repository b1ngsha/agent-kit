# Simplification patterns

Read when an unused-symbol search does not establish a candidate's value. These examples adapt the [upstream historical patterns](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/deepseek-harness/blob/master/.agents/skills/dsh-find-simplifications/references/historical-patterns.md) into repository-neutral trade-offs. Verify current owners and compatibility obligations before applying them.

## Require a complete effect path

A declaration, serializer branch, and test can suggest support without an effective feature. Trace both ends: something must produce the value, and an implementation must honor it. A reserved variant without an emitter or a request option every adapter rejects may disappear with its forwarding, rejection-only tests, and generated docs.

Zero fixed callers is weaker evidence than unreachability. A public registration function or generated catalog may serve third-party plugins through generic invocation. Prove the absence of an effective extension path before treating it as dead.

## Reduce distinctions where consumers decide

Map every public variant to the action it enables. Several internal routing or residency states may expose one usable target. Simplifying that projection can remove public registration methods, invalidation, disposal state, and combination tests while preserving the internal distinctions.

Durable start and completion markers may remain necessary for empty failed operations, crash repair, or inspection even when a renderer ignores them. Simplify the projection without erasing those facts.

## Trade general behavior for a smaller promise

Fixed-rate scheduling may remove calendar parsing, missed-occurrence replay, backlog states, and persisted variants. Explicit activation failure may remove automatic rollback while preserving failure reporting and consumer-owned activation checks. Moving rollback elsewhere retains its maintenance cost.

State the lost capability as clearly as the deletion. Truncating an interrupted operation may discard substantial real work; fewer states or tests alone do not justify it. A survey can propose the trade-off without authorizing its implementation.

## Make assumptions explicit at their consuming operation

An explicit time zone on a scheduling request may remove shared defaults, persistence, synchronization, and confirmation machinery. Caller-owned content framing may remove envelope controls threaded through unrelated layers.

Validation must remain at the boundary responsible for enforcement. Removing duplicate client parsing can be valid when the server still validates before lookup or business invocation, but delayed failure and any lost client redaction are behavior changes to name.

## Derive at use or reuse a delivery owner

A canonical history read at feedback time may replace a continuously copied buffer and listeners. Sorting current summaries may replace persisted recency ledgers. An existing projection stream may replace feature-specific notifications, repeated reads, and a second membership cache.

Timing decides whether the replacement holds: observation-time redaction differs from capture-time redaction; manual ordering is independent user state; a stream may need an initial history read when no baseline exists. Retain those obligations explicitly.

## Remove accidental composition owners

Trace actual launch and resolution paths behind directories called demo, example, or support. Duplicate compositions, resolver-only packages, and pass-through configuration may disappear when tests, guides, assets, and behavior join their existing owners. Explicit deployment policy may replace inference from optional service presence.

A smaller package count is insufficient. Separate operations may have different owners or completion timing, and an interface may intentionally serve external providers. Include remaining configuration and lifecycle glue in the comparison.

## Use rejected experiments to test parity

A parser dependency may delete generic framing and conformance tests while retaining a protocol-specific termination shim and accepting stricter truncated-tail behavior. A seemingly equivalent timer builtin may defeat deterministic fake-clock tests for little deletion. Size limits, teardown, platform packaging, and deployment behavior can make a nominal replacement more expensive.

A past rejection is a scoped falsifier, not a permanent blacklist. Revisit it with evidence addressing the failed premise; measure implementation, glue, tests, dependencies, and supported behavior together.
